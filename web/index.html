⚠️ What are Microplastics?

Microplastics are plastic particles smaller than 5 millimetres in size. They are classified as:

  • Primary microplastics — manufactured at micro-size (microbeads in cosmetics, industrial pellets)
  • Secondary microplastics — formed by breakdown of larger plastics through UV radiation, wave action, and weathering
💡 Fact: Over 8 million tonnes of plastic enter our oceans every year.

🔬 Morphology Types Detected

  • Fiber: From synthetic clothing (polyester, nylon) — released during washing
  • Fragment: From broken plastic items — bottles, containers, packaging
  • Pellet: Industrial nurdles — raw material used in plastic manufacturing
  • Film: From plastic bags, food wrappers, agricultural plastic mulch

📏 Size Classification Standards

  • Nanoplastic (< 1 µm): can cross cell membranes, enter bloodstream
  • Small Micro (1–100 µm): can be ingested by plankton, small fish
  • Large Micro (100–1000 µm): ingested by fish, seabirds, marine mammals
  • Mesoplastic (> 1000 µm): visible to naked eye, entanglement risk

🧬 Health Effects on Humans

Humans ingest an estimated 5 grams of microplastics per week — equivalent to a credit card.

  • Found in human blood, lung tissue, placenta, and colon
  • Carry toxic chemicals: BPA, phthalates, PCBs
  • Linked to inflammation, hormonal disruption, and cancer risk
⚠️ WHO (2019): Microplastics detected in 93% of bottled water samples globally.

🐋 Marine Ecosystem Impact

Marine life is severely affected at every level of the food chain:

  • Phytoplankton & Zooplankton: confuse microplastics for food, reducing caloric intake
  • Fish & Seabirds: gut blockage, toxin accumulation, 90% of seabird species affected
🌊 By 2050, there will be more plastic than fish in the ocean by weight.

✅ Solutions & Prevention

  • 🚫 Ban single-use plastics and microbeads in cosmetics
  • ♻️ Improve plastic recycling infrastructure globally
  • 🧪 Develop biodegradable plastic alternatives
  • 🏭 Install microplastic filters in wastewater treatment plants
