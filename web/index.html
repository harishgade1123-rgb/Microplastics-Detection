⚠️ What are Microplastics?

Microplastics are synthetic plastic particles smaller than 5 millimetres in size:

  • Primary microplastics: Manufactured at micro-scale (cosmetic microbeads, industrial pellets).
  • Secondary microplastics: Fragmentation of larger items via UV radiation, waves, and weathering.
💡 Fact: Over 8 million tonnes of plastic enter our oceans annually.

🔬 Morphology Types Detected

  • Fiber: Synthetic garments (polyester, nylon) shed during domestic laundry.
  • Fragment: Jagged debris from packaging, drink bottles, and rigid plastics.
  • Pellet: Industrial nurdles and pre-production raw polymer pellets.
  • Film: Ultra-thin sheets from bags, shrink wrap, and agricultural mulch.

📏 Size Classification Standards

  • Nanoplastic (< 1 µm): Penetrates cellular membranes, enters bloodstream.
  • Small Micro (1–100 µm): Ingested by plankton and small filter feeders.
  • Large Micro (100–1000 µm): Accumulated in fish, seabirds, marine life.
  • Mesoplastic (> 1000 µm): Visible to naked eye, entanglement risk.

🧬 Human Health Impact

Humans ingest an estimated 5 grams of microplastics per week — equivalent to a credit card.

  • Detected in human blood, lung tissue, placenta, and colon.
  • Leaches endocrine-disrupting chemicals: BPA, phthalates, and PFAS.
⚠️ WHO (2019): Detected in 93% of bottled water samples globally.
